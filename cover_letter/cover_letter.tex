Dear Professor Foster and members of the Committee,

I am delighted to apply for the position of Tenure Track Assistant Professor
advertised in your call for Faculty Positions in Computer and Communication
Sciences. I work in computational social choice, the study of voting and collective
decisions with the tools of computer science. I have proved complexity results, such as the hardness of deciding how
similar two elections are, and built the data and software needed to test
voting rules empirically. I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Geneva, hosted by Roland
Bouffanais and Didier Wernli. Before that, I was a CNRS postdoc at LAMSADE,
Paris Dauphine-PSL, with Jérôme Lang, and I received my PhD from AGH University of Kraków in 2024, advised by Piotr
Faliszewski.

My best-known contribution is the \emph{map of elections}. I proposed a
distance that compares elections regardless of how their candidates and voters
are labeled, which made it possible to place real and synthetic elections in a
common space and to see, for example, that real elections occupy only a small
part of it. The framework (\emph{Artificial Intelligence}, 2025) now extends to
approval elections, stable matching, tournaments, and fair division.
I also founded Pabulib, the largest
open library of participatory budgeting data, now cited in more than 100 papers. The team I belong to also convinced Polish
cities, including Wieliczka
and \'Swiecie, to adopt the Method of Equal Shares, a proportional voting rule.
In total, I have published 5 journal articles and 28 full conference papers,
mostly at IJCAI, AAMAS, AAAI, and NeurIPS. As a PhD student, I was the
principal investigator of a National Science Centre grant (NCN Preludium); my
thesis received the Polish Prime Minister's Award, and I also received a
START fellowship and the AAMAS 2023 Best Student Paper Award.

Collective decisions increasingly start from text: people write what they
think, and AI condenses it into a single summary. At EPFL, I will build the
tools to check whom such a summary represents, and whom it leaves out, for
example in a public consultation, and extend maps of elections to maps of
deliberations. In Geneva, I already work with votes over texts. I lead a study of all
101{,}070 abstentions in the UN General Assembly, with each resolution's
subject labeled by a language model, and I am a coauthor of a
simulation of democratic deliberation among LLM agents (Findings of EMNLP
2026). Computational social choice would be a new area at IC, and my agenda
shares questions with Bryan Ford's work on proof of personhood and e-voting,
Robert West's work on how language models shape online discourse, and Antoine
Bosselut's work on open language models such as Apertus. I would like to
continue this work in Switzerland, where I live and where citizens vote on
public questions several times a year.

At Jagiellonian University, I taught three undergraduate courses. I have also
presented the tutorial
\emph{Experiments in Social Choice} at IJCAI 2023, AAMAS 2023, and AAAI 2024,
and co-supervised more than ten BSc and MSc students, whose work led to papers
at AAAI 2025 and ECAI 2025. At EPFL, I can teach core bachelor courses such as
programming, discrete mathematics, and algorithms. In my own courses, students
will apply each concept to real data, and I would like to develop
a master course on algorithms for collective decisions and a seminar on AI and
democracy.

Please find enclosed my CV, publication list, research statement, and teaching
statement; my references are listed in the application. Thank you for
considering my application.

Yours sincerely,\\[4pt]
Stanisław Szufa
